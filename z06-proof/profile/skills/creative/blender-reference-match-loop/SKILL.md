# Blender reference-match craft

Apply Blender-specific geometry, receiver, mapping and integration methods under a locked visual contract. Use dream-loop v2 for project-agnostic target/owner/judge policy when available; this pack supplies the craft, not a competing promotion rule. A single matched still is not a relightable digital twin.

## When to Use

- Match an editable Blender scene to a reference; diagnose visible geometry/material/lighting failures; port approved craft to sibling variants.
- For read-only critique, inspect existing captures and receipts only. Do not render or modify scenes.

## Prerequisites

Discover the actual Blender executable/version and product root. Read the selected fixture, target, BRIEF, canonical scoped OWNER-PREFERENCES, prior failures and protected-state contract. Paste applicable owner decisions into every worker/judge packet. Resolve one shared GPU admission/lock mechanism; do not hardcode another project's lock path.

## How to Run

Use `read_file` for receipts and scripts, `terminal` with explicit workdir for bounded headless Blender jobs, and `vision_analyze` with native regions for actual output. Use `--python-exit-code 1`; isolate inherited Python environment variables if they contaminate Blender. Keep scripts/inputs/outputs in the product's owned directories.

## Procedure

1. **Freeze identity and baseline.** Resolve CURRENT explicitly; preserve target/source hashes, camera/pose, output density, view/look/exposure, lighting and compositor. Reproduce a no-change control. Protect only the owner's named attributes; silhouette lock does not freeze every shader/pixel. Scene state equality is not pixel determinism.
2. **Approve style before production.** Name ART STYLE and COPY/INTENT, and decompose the style into layers separate from materials (film grain, halftone/print screen, ink lines, cel/tone bands, low-poly faceting). Read the shading per unit before naming a material: one flat tone per facet means low-poly game-style geometry, a smooth gradient means rounded forms; a wrong read (e.g. "rounded wet cobbles" for faceted slate) burns whole iterations. Ask the owner how they read the style when unsure. Supply native target crop, structural reading, two different causal form constructions, negative example and real coupon; owner approves before full asset work or scatter. Hold the objective fixed while testing representations. See [materials from reference](references/materials-from-reference.md).
3. **Preflight the actual receiver.** Ray-test render-visible evaluated geometry; inspect effective OBJECT/DATA slots, reachable shader branches, UV/tangent domains, modifiers, normals, occlusion and inherited contributors. Check light-link receiver membership after replacement and exposure/view/glare before rendering. A node count or hidden object cannot prove the visible surface changed. Before dispatching a trial that applies an approved material or asset, confirm the receiver does not already use it; otherwise the trial silently becomes a different experiment.
4. **Scan the whole frame, then diagnose.** Start every round with a breadth-first difference scan of the full frame against the target, region by region: presence and coverage of growth and props, blur/bokeh per depth band, light sources (points vs strands vs discs), luminance, and stray artifacts (floating geometry, hard mask/slab edges). Rank differences by visible area x severity / cost and fix the cheap large ones before any deep single-element lane: an element lane only sees its own element, so obvious misses elsewhere survive many rounds until the owner points them out. Drive placement from the target itself: camera-project target-derived masks (greenery, light positions) onto the scene geometry as scatter density/weight maps so growth lands exactly where the reference has it. Then classify FORM, MATERIAL RESPONSE, LIGHT, ALBEDO, MAPPING or DISPLAY. Before naming the response a region needs (wet sheen, clear coat, brighter moss), measure it on matched ROIs in TARGET and CURRENT (low-pass luminance median, hue, local contrast, highlight count/shape) and write the direction of change down. Dark, low-contrast regions misread by eye in either direction, and a mechanism built from a wrong read (a sheen on a face the target renders darker) burns whole rounds before a reviewer notices. Compute region statistics on the pixels of the receiver you will change (ray-tested or object-index mask), not a hand-drawn box: a nominated box can sample a neighbouring block and send the lane after the wrong surface. In dark scenes take colour statistics only above a luminance floor; raw chroma medians dominated by near-black pixels can call a saturated target "less colourful". Test a global tone/colour hypothesis in image space first (grade the CURRENT render PNG, then two side-swapped reviewers against the reference): it costs no render and kills a wrong read before any light or compositor change. Before choosing what to work on, map the element's visible surfaces by share of frame area in the target and compare each with ours: rounds spent on a surface seen at a grazing angle (a plinth top) cannot fix a large flat face (its front) that dominates how the element reads. For silhouette/flush/step problems use [human-like geometry edits](references/geometry-edits-like-a-human.md) and [ray probe](scripts/camera_ray_probe.py). Trace edges, fit permitted rigid dimensions/contact constraints, and disclose inconsistent generated perspective rather than warping protected tops. Small exact owner edits deserve a short direct script and isolation, not an expensive competition.
5. **Research and falsify.** Ground nontrivial operations in current APIs and artist explanations. Build a one-variable coupon/control, then apply in the integrated fixture. Develop a material/form family in an ISOLATION copy: hide_render everything but the subject, keep camera, world/view and its light rig, show protected decals as matte placeholders, and compare against the target with non-subject regions masked; re-enable the full scene only after the owner picks. Use neutral diagnostics to explain mechanisms, production lighting to accept them. One hypothesis may have explicitly coupled controls; do not change unrelated classes.
6. **Gate and judge honestly.** If a frame is too dark or too bright to judge materials, first match the reference's region luminance percentiles with lights/exposure only, then judge. Calibrate composition gates per target family; failed hard gates permit structural diagnosis unless the owner authorizes scoped local diagnostics. After PASS do not optimize insignificant gate fractions. Log self-review/directive state, then fresh blind A/B with sides swapped, approved deviations and delivered native dimensions. Owner-calibrate rankers; keep technical/brief/owner gates separate. See [tiled criticism](references/tiled-critic.md). For deep zoom or oversized final output, require the [final-density gate](references/final-density.md); review-sized images do not certify it. Render and review every candidate twice: LOOK (DOF/haze/bloom fitted to the reference, judged for appearance) and CLEAR (no DOF, judged for craft and final density); a crisp render can miss the target's look and a blurred one hides defects. Fitting DOF is allowed when camera transform and lens are locked; see [locked optics](references/locked-optics.md).
7. **Integrate and preserve.** Fix exposure/lighting first, then materials, form/props, dependent attachments and one bloom owner. Apply idempotently to a frozen copy; save/reopen and verify exact protected geometry/UVs/material graphs plus measured appearance. Preserve required zero-user materials with fake users; do not weaken guards to hide save-time loss. With parallel component lanes, admit each component into the selected scene only through an in-scene side-swapped A/B against the CURRENT selection at that moment: components that win in isolation often tie or lose once composed. Fix one promotion bar for every lane before dispatch (for example both side-swapped reviewers prefer, no new named defect, measurements move toward the target) and back up the selection file before each change; lanes that invent a stricter or looser bar diverge silently. When both reviewers prefer a candidate but one measured regression blocks it, repair only that variable on a copy instead of starting a new family; if the repair exposes a new defect, stop that line and present the tradeoff as an owner choice. Recombination of partial wins (coverage from one trial, volume from another, the literal approved assets from the baseline) is often the step that finally wins.
8. **Port deliberately.** Share approved geometry/quality through one layer plus variant config. Compare mesh/UV hashes and visible inventories, not names/matrices alone. Preserve donor polygon material indices and theme display transforms; port lighting as split shared/theme/view recipes. A no-layer control separates base drift from port credit. Freeze geometry before dependent decals and re-seat them along original rays. See [porting](references/porting.md).
9. **Checkpoint and stop.** Show TARGET | previous | candidate and native interfaces in the owner's viewer after every batch; verify delivery. On a shared desktop, verify the review in an owned headless/nested browser with a separate profile, not by opening or focusing the owner's browser. Follow focus-safe-desktop-automation, record host focus/workspaces before and after, verify the live title and image loads, capture the owned viewport and close only that owned process. Foreground presentation requires explicit authorization (a standing owner preference to open reviews in a named browser counts; then verify the window title); an HTML delivery alone is not permission to steal focus. When delivering a tool the owner must operate (rating app, viewer), open it last so it is the focused tab and lead the message with it as the single requested action: a tool opened among several review tabs goes unseen. Keep rejected/parked features and combine owner picks on current lineage. Two failed trials on a stable defect block another near-variant; renaming a parameter/family does not reset it. Before the next dispatch on that defect, run a Dream-RSI step over the trial archive (dream-loop's dream step, or by hand): untried mechanism classes, recombinations of what different trials got right, recipes proven on other elements, one wildcard; each with its cheapest coupon test. Present those directions instead of another variant of the failed family. Keep open owner decisions as one short numbered list, each with a recommended option, and restate it at every checkpoint: questions buried in long asynchronous updates get missed. Exception: when the owner has explicitly asked for open exploration ("try both", "be experimental"), a failed family switches to a genuinely different mechanism inside the running batch instead of halting; run several distinct mechanisms per batch (at least four), escalate winners, and present the whole portfolio at the end. Record every owner verdict as a structured label (trial id, accept/reject or stars, scope, note) in the trial ledger at the moment it is given: decisions left only in chat are invisible to any learner or calibrated judge, which then reports zero accepted observations. If the ledger is empty, backfill it from the owner's chat history: verbatim quote, session/message id and timestamp, mapping evidence (what was shown just before the message, any attached screenshot) and a mapping confidence; bind to a trial only when unambiguous, otherwise record element-level scope instead of guessing, keep praise and complaints from one message as separate aspects, and let later explicit owner ratings supersede backfills. A HOLD does not suppress the checkpoint; a budget is a ceiling, not mandatory burn.

## Pitfalls

- Diagnose missing reflection sources, occlusion, light-link exclusions and display transforms before roughness/energy tuning. A linked light can still spill through indirect bounces; measure protected pixels and qualify a scoped bounce limit.
- Distinguish artwork UVs from metric detail coordinates. Never box-project tangent RGB normals without basis reorientation. Verify the effective consumer before claiming a UV fix landed.
- For dense front-face relief, keep top and front coverage separate, range-limit conformal label rays, and evaluate copied-object transforms before hashing; see [dense relief and labels](references/dense-relief-and-labels.md) for tested Blender 5.2 instance, compositor and tiled-image pitfalls.
- For locked-camera DOF, haze, selective background blur, paired CLEAR controls and save-time palette retention, follow [locked optics](references/locked-optics.md); premask before diffusion and inspect bright silhouettes at native pixels.
- Use new capture filenames; stale caches can show an old mesh. Reopen in a fresh process when repeat-render drift appears, preserving both receipts.
- Treat shared-render queue time as distinct from Blender render time. When a foreground tool times out, inspect the exact lane's process cwd and output log before retrying: the queued renderer may still exist. Wait for that job rather than launching a duplicate; never kill another lane's Blender or bypass the shared lock.
- Probe the final visible ground receiver's height before judging a landscape pass. A new lawn below an existing plaza is invisible, and coplanar intersecting walk slabs can produce hard black z-fighting rectangles; separate their surfaces by a small explicit metric offset.
- Before setting state-driven light watts, initialize the authoritative scene properties and evaluate them during the same fresh build. A driver created before its custom scene property exists can render at zero yet appear valid after reopening the saved blend; compare real pixel deltas and inspect both fresh-build and reopen paths. For build-per-clock authoring, a post-state resolver is simpler and more reliable than an early driver.
- Freeze and hash the executable scripts before queuing a long render. A module already loaded in Blender does not change when its file is edited; hashing that live file later misidentifies the executed source. Archive the loaded version and report any transient on-disk hash separately instead of claiming source/render identity.
- Separate valid geometry, visible mechanism, approved style and held-out transfer. More hair, droplets, noise or samples cannot fix the wrong morphology. Likewise, density/leaf/dot changes scattered on a flat support cannot create growth that reads as a voluminous lumpy mass in the target: when rounds of surface scatter tie the baseline, the missing piece is the mass and silhouette, so change the representation rather than the scatter.
- Qualify the botanical instance itself at native and deepest zoom before filling a canopy. A dense opaque scatter can hide every stone curl yet turn folded, low-vertex leaf fans into crystal chips and a uniform green carpet. Crisp geometry is not leaf fidelity. Keep coverage, native leaf identity, mass/negative-space variation and final-density craft as separate gates; preserve the approved leaf asset language when broadening placement. The construction that wins combines three separately sourced layers: coverage from a target-derived region mask (subject's face excluded), real volume underneath shaped by the target silhouette with an irregular inner edge, and the literal approved leaf meshes and materials instanced over that volume at several depths (darker inside, lit outer layer) with light chains threaded through.
- Scope projection, external assets and AI imagery separately and follow the current OWNER-PREFERENCES; a historical approved fixed-view projection is not general permission. This owner rejects 2D AI paint-overs of renders. AI-generated images are acceptable only when projection-painted onto meshes and baked into textures inside Blender. When the shaded reference itself is projected as a low-frequency tone band under geometric and procedural detail, strip light-source contributions first (emitter glow and spill from nearby lights such as bead strands): they survive any low-pass and render as smooth glowing patches that hide the surface; project an albedo-only band and let the scene lights do the lighting.
- For deep-zoom deliverables, budget fidelity at the deepest zoom, not at the review render size. Camera-projected sources (Artwork UV art, projected hero props, reference-derived plates) can never exceed the reference's resolution, so at 5-12x zoom they blur. Target at least 1 texel per final pixel and render fixed border crops at full final resolution every round. Prefer geometry, object/UV-space procedurals and vectorised graphic art. For printed graphics (lettering, stripes, logos, microtype) ask the owner for the original vector files before tracing: tracing a super-resolved, descreened texture with corner-aware curve fitting and edge-distance affine registration brings simple shapes under one source pixel, but a near-match font changes the letterforms and microtype cannot be recovered at all. Screen-space (Window-coordinate) halftone or grain changes size with render resolution, so define it in object/UV space. When the shared GPU lacks VRAM for full-density border crops, render them on Cycles CPU rather than shrinking textures, which would fake the result.
- Before building a print/engraving pattern layer, measure the target's stroke statistics from native crops (angle histogram, run lengths, spacing, coverage versus tone) and modulate stroke width and gaps continuously. Repeating tile sets (Truchet or maze tiles) read as wallpaper and periodic lattices as chain-link; switching whole stroke bundles on and off by tone reads as sparse chevrons.
- Before removing a low-resolution source image to reach max-zoom fidelity, check what it carries: a portrait/print image often supplies the anatomy that coarse relief geometry lacks, and deleting it turns the subject into a crude faceted mask. Keep it band-limited instead (descreen and blur to its true information content, which stays clean at any zoom) and put crisp object/UV-space detail layers (ink, engraving, grain) on top.
- Components proven in isolation (form, tone, pattern, AI-painted UDIM wear, coat, grain) are not judged until they are composed as a real layer stack: mix by cavity/AO/curvature/height/facing masks, keep colour spaces correct, blend normals properly (reoriented/UDN), keep coat/roughness as separate layers, and try several stacks including unexpected combinations. A layer that vanishes at hero size (a fine nested pattern) can still be the one that carries max zoom. Use AI projection-painted UDIMs for bounded wear and micro-detail in cavities, not for anatomy or main carving, where cross-view disagreement and invented marks show.

## Verification

Require immutable inputs, no-change baseline, receiver/fixture receipt, actual candidate/control output, save/reopen/idempotence guards, native/final-density review, owner checkpoint and exact selection/approval status. For movable/relightable deliverables test authorized off-axis/light variants against their own contract, not a nonexistent target view.
