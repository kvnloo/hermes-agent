# Regional ink over fixed relief

## When to Use

Use when a carved face already has physical relief and an inherited printed material, but anatomy and engraving are hard to judge separately.

1. Freeze the scene and inspect the actual receiver material slot, UV, Base Color consumer and normal/roughness/coat/emission branches. Do not infer absent ink from a small LOOK frame; inspect native CLEAR.
2. Render matched BASE, TONE, INK and RELIEF controls before composing. For true neutral-clay diagnostics, explicitly disconnect inherited roughness, normal, coat and emission inputs as well as Base Color; assigning defaults does nothing while inputs remain linked. State that fixed geometry remains present in material-only controls.
3. Separate broad landmark pigment masks from engraving. A low-frequency image interpreted from a small registered portrait is not recovered microgeometry or gigapixel anatomy. If tone-only still has a blob eyelid or faceted brow, retain that failure in the report.
4. Test genuinely different constructions. Analytical direction fields can make an eye bullseye and cheek stripes even when called form-following. Reject that family after two assessed failures and run the archive's mechanism-switch process rather than tuning it again.
5. Recombine only regional partial features. A local nested motif plus existing hero ink is not authorization to apply a global wallpaper or spread one pattern across every facial tone.
6. Freeze the exact application module with its SHA256 beside each rendered condition. If diagnostics change after a prior render, preserve the earlier source rather than pointing all receipts at the newest mutable module.
7. Check idempotence through both owned graph digest and datablock counts; guard meshes, UVs, transforms, lighting and non-owned materials. Preserve image/material users needed across save/reopen.
8. Use actual final-density borders at fixed physical coordinates. Include the changed motif and its mask boundary, not only convenient face landmarks. A sampled UV Jacobian minimum-axis texel bound is not exhaustive visible coverage and does not certify inherited textures or whole-banner fidelity.
9. Label sampling and optics differences. Matched coupons can settle material attribution; historical full frames at another sample count cannot establish pixel-exact preservation. Keep native craft, phone readability, target match, technical preservation and owner approval separate.
10. Under shared render locking, an empty log plus a waiting flock process means queued, not a failed material or completed render. Never bypass the lock or terminate another lane. Record actual subprocess exits and reopen the selected artifact before claiming integration.
11. Validate event schemas before appending planning records. Preserve unsupported original planning data when normalizing it, and do not count a planning envelope as a rendered trial.

Evidence basis: matched real CPU coupon ablations, deterministic landmark maps, rejected analytical-eye patterns, frozen application-source receipts, and six actual final-density border inspections. These lessons do not establish a universal visual winner or held-out owner calibration.
